Every abstraction is a promise that the thing it hides is uglier than the hole it leaves behind. Some keep that promise. Some just kick the complexity sideways.

**The test I use: can I delete the boundary in an afternoon?** Leaky abstractions usually fail here — they couple to call sites, to config, to the shape of the data.

## Boundaries that earn their keep

- They shrink the vocabulary of the calling code.
- They concentrate the change in one place.
- You can point at the moment they paid for themselves.

## The building problem

A library is a building: it has rooms, load-bearing walls, and a footprint you cannot dig out from under. Keep dependencies at the edges. A dependency high in the tree is a building; a dependency at the leaves is a tool.
