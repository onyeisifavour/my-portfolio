A rewrite is a long silence with a big payout at the end. The silence is the problem: no feedback, no milestone that feels real, nobody to tell you it still matters.

This time I promised myself every step would ship. Not a branch — a release.

## The smallest shippable slice

Each feature was cut until it was barely a feature. The first release of the new CLI did exactly one thing: read a config file and print it back. That shipped on day three.

After that, every week had a cadence: pick the next ugly spot, make it small, ship it.

## What broke

- I cut a test suite *and* rewrote the parser in the same slice. Two rewrites in one step is one rewrite too many.
- A "small" step that touched the public API took three days of churn. Small on the inside is not the same as small on the outside.
- Not shipping when a step was 60% there cost more than the 40% ever saved.

It is slower until it is faster. The boring parts carried the project; the flashy parts mostly got in the way.
