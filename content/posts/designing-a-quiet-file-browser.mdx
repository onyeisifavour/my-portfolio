A file browser has one job: show people what is here and let them move it around. The harder part is keeping that calm when there are hundreds of nodes on screen.

**Start with the shortest plausible hierarchy.** Every folder level you add is a decision the user must make. If a tree can be flattened, flatten it. If a tab can be a filter, make it a filter.

## Hairlines do the walking

The lines between rows carry more information than they appear to. A hairline at 10% ink reads as *structure*; at 30% it turns into *objects*. We kept every separator at `10%` and let indentation, not color, carry depth.

Whitespace is the same trick in reverse. The empty band between the sidebar and the listing isn't decoration — it's where the eye learns which pane it is looking at.

## Why every border says something

Once every border is a hairline, anything thicker becomes a signal. Errors, selection, drag targets. That is the whole point of being quiet everywhere else: the loud parts stay loud.
